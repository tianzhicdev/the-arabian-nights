Chernyshevsky: [settling back, reflective] There is a scene in the second part—the encounter with the prostitute Liza. [pause] I think it is the heart of the entire book.

Nietzsche: [interested, leaning forward] Tell me how you read it.

Chernyshevsky: [beginning slowly, storytelling tone] The Underground Man has just humiliated himself at a dinner party. His old schoolmates mocked him, ignored him, left him behind when they went to a brothel. [wry] So naturally, he follows them. Arrives hours late, drunk, raging. They have already gone.

Nietzsche: [quietly] And he stays.

Chernyshevsky: [nodding] He stays. [pause] And there he meets Liza—a young woman, twenty years old, new to the profession. [sad] Not yet hardened. Still capable of shame.

Nietzsche: [softly] Still capable of hope.

Chernyshevsky: [building, more animated] Exactly. And the Underground Man sees this—sees her vulnerability—and he begins to speak. [shifting to dramatic tone] He describes her future with terrible precision. The disease that will come. The degradation.

Nietzsche: [grim appreciation] He is eloquent.

Chernyshevsky: [emphatic] Devastatingly eloquent.
