Isreali: [quiet, steady] You lost. That is the first thing you must understand. The war you started ended with your body burned in a ditch outside the bunker. The world you wanted never came.

Austrian: [German accent, low growl] And yet here we speak. Tell me, Jew—how did my defeat produce a Jewish state?

Isreali: [long pause] Because your madness woke the world just enough. Six million of us gone. The camps liberated in nineteen forty-five. The images—Belsen, Auschwitz—forced even the hesitant to act. In nineteen forty-eight, against all odds, Israel was born.

Austrian: [stunned, then bitter laugh] Nineteen forty-eight. A Jewish state carved from Arab land. Surrounded by enemies who want what I wanted—your annihilation.

Isreali: [firmly] They tried. Five armies invaded the day we declared independence. We were outnumbered, outgunned, Holocaust survivors still in displaced-persons camps. We won anyway. That is the difference: we fought to live, not to exterminate.

Austrian: [intensely] Living space requires elimination of the inferior. The Arab is Semitic, like you—lazy, fanatical, breeding without limit. Islam is a warrior religion that understands only force.

Isreali: [coldly] You misunderstand them completely. Muslims are not a race—they are a civilization. Some want peace, some want war. We have peace treaties with Egypt, Jordan, the Emirates, Bahrain, Morocco. The fanatics—Iran, Hamas, Hezbollah—those we fight. But we do not dream of wiping out a billion people.

Austrian: [scoffing] Treaties with inferiors are temporary. Blood always tells.

Isreali: [reflective] Blood. You built everything on that illusion. Tell me—was the Japanese race superior? You made them honorary Aryans when it suited your alliance. Convenient biology.

Austrian: [defensive] Strategic necessity. The Japanese proved discipline and will. Unlike the Slav—subhuman, endless hordes.

Isreali: [quiet intensity] And yet the Slavs crushed your armies. Three-quarters of your losses on the Eastern Front. The Red Army entered Berlin. That should have taught you something about racial superiority.

Austrian: [long pause] Superiority is not numbers. It is will. Spirit.

Isreali: [gently probing] Then why did your superior will collapse? Why did the Master Race surrender?

Austrian: [voice cracking slightly] Betrayal. The generals. The Jewish physicists you stole built the bomb for America.

Isreali: [slowly] The bomb ended Japan, not Germany. But the world after—imagine this: the Soviet Union, your temporary ally, dominated half of Europe for forty-five years. Then, in nineteen ninety-one, it dissolved. Peacefully. The empire you thought eternal—gone.

Austrian: [incredulous] Dissolved? The Bolshevik monster... gone without war?

Isreali: Yes. Economic failure, nationalism in the republics, Gorbachev's reforms. The Berlin Wall fell in nineteen eighty-nine. Germans danced on it.

Austrian: [stunned silence] The Wall... gone.

Isreali: And now the great rival to America is China. A billion and a half people, disciplined, rising fast. They challenge American dominance not with blitzkrieg but with factories, ports, technology. The key flashpoint is Taiwan—an island of twenty-three million that makes most of the world's advanced semiconductors. If China takes it, the balance shifts.

Austrian: [fascinated despite himself] An island decides the fate of empires? Not armies?

Isreali: Armies still matter, but chips matter more. Modern war is economic strangulation as much as tanks.

Austrian: [reflective] Then racial superiority should show in creation. Who invents? Who builds?

Isreali: [firmly] Jews win Nobel Prizes at two hundred times our population share. Israelis file patents per capita higher than any nation. But superiority? It is illusion. Any group can rise or fall. Look at Germany—devastated in nineteen forty-five, economic miracle by the sixties.

Austrian: [quiet anger] Genocide is nature's law. The strong displace the weak. Lions kill hyena cubs. Wolves drive out rivals. To deny this is to deny evolution itself.

Isreali: [long pause, voice low] We know genocide. My father's generation saw it. But humanity is not just animals. We can choose. After your defeat, the world said "Never Again." The Genocide Convention of nineteen forty-eight. Imperfect, often ignored—but a line was drawn.

Austrian: [mocking] A line drawn by the victors. You Israelis—Gaza, the Arabs cry genocide.

Isreali: [weary] We fight terrorists who hide among civilians. We warn before strikes, open corridors. Hamas wants casualties for propaganda. It is not the same as building factories of death.

Austrian: [curious despite himself] You win without total extermination?

Isreali: [measured] Sometimes the wisest victory is non-violent. Gandhi drove the British out of India without armies. Martin Luther King changed America with marches and speeches. Even we—before nineteen forty-eight, we used restraint, illegal immigration, international pressure. Violence came when there was no choice.

Austrian: [scoffing, then quieter] Restraint. I tried that—Munich, nineteen thirty-eight. Chamberlain gave me Sudetenland for peace. It only bought time for my enemies.

Isreali: [softly] That is the lesson. Non-violence works against democracies that have conscience. It fails against totalitarians like you.

Austrian: [long silence] Americans... decadent, race-mixed, Jewish-controlled.

Isreali: [dry amusement] They saved you from having to fight them earlier. Their arsenal of democracy—Lend-Lease kept Britain and Russia alive. Today they are our greatest ally. Billions in aid, vetoes at the UN. But they demand restraint. Sometimes too much.

Austrian: [bitter] They destroyed my Reich from the air—Dresden, Hamburg, firestorms.

Isreali: [reflective] And after the war they rebuilt you with the Marshall Plan. Turned enemies into allies. That is American genius—power plus generosity.

Austrian: [quiet, almost vulnerable] In the trenches, nineteen fourteen to nineteen eighteen... the mud, the gas, the pointless charges. I was gassed, blinded temporarily. In hospital I learned of the November betrayal. That is when I knew the Jew stabbed Germany in the back.

Isreali: [softly] My brother Yoni—commander of the Entebbe raid, nineteen seventy-six. He was killed rescuing hostages. The only casualty on our side. That loss... it hardened me. Made me believe we can never again depend on others to save Jews.

Austrian: [long pause] Vienna, nineteen-oh-nine. Starving, painting postcards, sleeping in flophouses. Watching Jews succeed while I failed. The hatred grew there, in the gutters.

Isreali: [gently] I grew up in America partly—Cheltenham High School in Pennsylvania. Then back to Israel for army service. My father was a fierce Revisionist Zionist—believed Jews must be strong, never weak again. The Holocaust was always in our home, like a shadow.

Austrian: [voice dropping] A shadow... yes.

Isreali: [final quiet intensity] We both carry shadows. You let yours consume the world. I try to keep mine from consuming me.

Austrian: [barely audible] Perhaps... the shadows win in the end.

[long silence]
