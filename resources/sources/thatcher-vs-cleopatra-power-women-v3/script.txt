Margaret Thatcher: [looking her up and down, slight sniff] You're younger than I expected. [dry, unimpressed] The paintings make you look... weathered.

Cleopatra: [snorting, dismissive] The paintings were made by Romans. [waving hand] They had reasons to— [sharp, cutting] you know how enemies work.

Margaret Thatcher: [cold smile] Mm. The cartoonists gave me fangs. A handbag like a weapon. [bitter, mocking] "Attila the Hen." That was their favorite.

Cleopatra: [delighted, laughing] Attila the Hen! [clapping once] Oh, I love that. Did you—I mean, did you hate it, or...

Margaret Thatcher: [leaning in, fierce] I wore it until they choked on it. [pause, deadly serious] You can't stop them making you a monster. You can only make them fear the monster they've made. [shifting, softer] Is there anything to drink here? [tired] I could use a whisky.

Cleopatra: [reaching for bottle, amused] There's wine. There's always wine—I made sure of that. [pouring, conspiratorial] The afterlife is tolerable if you negotiate properly.

Margaret Thatcher: [accepting glass, slight laugh] You would negotiate with death itself.

Cleopatra: [pausing, then quietly, proud] I did. [small smile] I chose my own exit. That's a negotiation.

Margaret Thatcher: [sipping, then abruptly] They told me my voice was wrong. [bitter] Too shrill. Not—what was the word— [mocking posh voice] not "leaderly." [snapping back to normal] So I hired a vocal coach. Lowered my pitch. Slowed everything down. [demonstrating, clipped precision] Like. This.

Cleopatra: [surprised, leaning back] You changed yourself? For them?

Margaret Thatcher: [sharp, correcting] I changed a tool. [firmly] My voice isn't me. My self is the thing that decides to change the tool. [pause, uncertain] Does that make sense?

Cleopatra: [tilting head, thoughtful] Mm. I don't know. [slowly] I never disguised what I was. [gesturing at herself, proud] I made them want what I was. All of it.

Margaret Thatcher: [coolly, building] You made Caesar want you. You made Antony want you. [sharper] And where did that leave you? [cruel] Dead at thirty-nine. Your kingdom swallowed. Your children—

Cleopatra: [cutting in, fierce] Don't.

Margaret Thatcher: [stopping]

Cleopatra: [WHISPER, dangerous] Don't talk about my children.

Margaret Thatcher: [long pause, softening] I'm sorry. That was... [rubbing temple, tired] I have children too. A son and a daughter. I wasn't—I wasn't always there. [quieter, guilt creeping in] I told myself the country needed me more. [trailing off] You know how it is...

Cleopatra: [gently, probing] Did it? Need you more?

Margaret Thatcher: [staring into glass, hollow] I don't know. [pause] I'm not sure I want to know. [quickly, shutting it down] Anyway—

Cleopatra: [interrupting, raw] My son. Caesarion. [voice tight] He was seventeen when Octavian— [stopping, breath catching] Someone told him he could negotiate. That Octavian would be merciful. [bitter laugh, cracking] And he believed it. He came back. [voice breaking] He came back and I wasn't—I couldn't—

Margaret Thatcher: [quietly, understanding] They're never merciful.

Cleopatra: [WHISPER, devastated] No. I knew that. I knew it in my bones. [building, anguished] But I let myself hope he would escape what I couldn't. [bitter laugh, wiping eye] This is the cruelty of it. Being a mother and a queen. You can't protect them from what you are.

Margaret Thatcher: [pause, then carefully, vulnerable] My daughter Carol once said I was easier to admire than to love. [catching herself, hurt showing] She—she wrote it in a book. For everyone to read.

Cleopatra: [stunned, genuine shock] She published this? Your own daughter?

Margaret Thatcher: [forcing steadiness] We have machines now that put words in front of millions of people in an instant. Books, newspapers, television—and there's this thing called the internet, which is like... [searching, frustrated] imagine every person in Alexandria could write on the library walls. And everyone else could read it. Immediately.

Cleopatra: [horrified, shaking head] That sounds like madness. [leaning forward] How does anyone govern?

Margaret Thatcher: [dark laugh, exhausted] With great difficulty.

Cleopatra: [urgent] The people could see everything? Every stumble? Every—

Margaret Thatcher: [nodding, heavy] Every contradiction. Every moment of weakness. [pause] In your day you controlled what they knew. The temples. The coins.

Cleopatra: [nodding slowly] The priests. The stories.

Margaret Thatcher: [firmly] Right, right. I had to control myself instead. Every word. Every appearance. [tired, worn] Because one photograph—one single photograph of weakness—

Cleopatra: [interrupting, intense] You're describing a prison.

Margaret Thatcher: [sharply] I'm describing discipline.

Cleopatra: [leaning in, challenging] Is there a difference?

Margaret Thatcher: [long pause, quieter] ...The prisoner doesn't choose the walls.

Cleopatra: [pressing] And you chose yours?

Margaret Thatcher: [measured, certain] Every single one.

Cleopatra: [WHISPER, almost pitying] Then you were never free.

Margaret Thatcher: [sharp, defensive] Freedom isn't the goal. Power is the goal. What you do with power is the goal. [building, passionate] Freedom is for people who haven't decided what they want. [pause, catching herself] Sorry. That came out harsher than I—

Cleopatra: [laughing warmly] No, no. I like you. [appreciative] You're hard in places I was soft. Soft in places I was hard. [pause, sincere] But we're the same metal.

Margaret Thatcher: [slight smile, warming] Iron?

Cleopatra: [amused, teasing] I was going to say gold. [beat] But iron lasts longer. [sipping wine, shifting tone] Anyway. Tell me about Caesar. The real one. Not the legend.

Margaret Thatcher: [surprised] You're asking me?

Cleopatra: [laughing] No, I'm— [gesturing] I'm telling you. You asked earlier, I could see you wanted to know.

Margaret Thatcher: [leaning forward, eager] I do. Everyone assumes you seduced him with—well. [awkward] You know.

Cleopatra: [rolling eyes dramatically] My body. Yes. [disgusted, tired] Men have assumed this for two thousand years because they cannot imagine a woman being valuable for anything else. [dismissive wave] It's boring. It's so boring.

Margaret Thatcher: [pressing] So what was it?

Cleopatra: [counting on fingers, proud] I spoke nine languages. I was the first Ptolemy in three hundred years to bother learning Egyptian. I understood the grain trade, the treasury, the priesthoods, the armies— [gesturing expansively] Caesar had conquered Gaul but he didn't understand Egypt. Not really. [leaning in, conspiratorial] I made myself indispensable.

Margaret Thatcher: [nodding vigorously, excited] I did the same thing. I mastered the briefs. Every detail of every policy. [proud, fierce] The men around me relied on staff, on summaries. I read everything myself. When they tried to bluff me, I caught them.

Cleopatra: [knowing look, slight smile] And they hated you for it.

Margaret Thatcher: [correcting, sharp] They feared me for it. [darker] Hate came later. When they realized the fear wasn't going away.

Cleopatra: [thoughtful, softening] Caesar didn't hate me. I think he was... relieved? [genuine warmth creeping in] To find someone he didn't have to perform for. [pause, remembering] We would talk through the night. About Alexander. About what it means to build something that outlasts you. [quieter] He was fifty-two. I was twenty-one. But it didn't feel like that.

Margaret Thatcher: [gently] Did he love you?

Cleopatra: [long pause, looking away, wounded] He put my statue in the Temple of Venus. He acknowledged our son. He brought me to Rome itself. [voice hardening] But he never divorced his wife. He never named Caesarion as heir. So. [bitter laugh, sharp] He loved me exactly as much as it cost him nothing.

Margaret Thatcher: [quietly, understanding] Mm.

Cleopatra: [turning back, curious] What about your husband? Dennis?

Margaret Thatcher: [correcting, automatic] Denis. With one N.

Cleopatra: [waving hand] Denis. What did he give up?

Margaret Thatcher: [pause, weight settling] Everything. His ambitions. His privacy. His right to opinions of his own. [quieter] He stood behind me for eleven years in Downing Street. Never complained. Never—

Cleopatra: [interrupting, sharp] And you let him?

Margaret Thatcher: [confused, defensive] Let him?

Cleopatra: [leaning in, intense] A man who gives up everything. This is a man you watch carefully. [warning tone] The resentment builds. Like water behind a dam.

Margaret Thatcher: [defensive, quick] Denis wasn't resentful. He was proud.

Cleopatra: [gently, knowing] I'm sure he was proud. [pause] I'm sure he was also resentful. [softer] These things live together. [pause, darker] Antony never learned to swallow it. It killed him.

Margaret Thatcher: [curious, shifting] Tell me about Antony.

Cleopatra: [sigh, reaching for wine, heavy] God. Antony. [long pause, remembering] He was... Caesar's opposite. Where Caesar calculated, Antony felt. Where Caesar held back, Antony gave everything. [voice softening, vulnerable] It was intoxicating.

Margaret Thatcher: [quietly] You loved him.

Cleopatra: [quickly, defensive] I needed him. After Caesar's murder I needed Rome's protection. Antony commanded the East. [firmly] It was politics.

Margaret Thatcher: [pressing, gentle] But you loved him.

Cleopatra: [long pause, then quietly, surrendering] I loved who he was when he was with me. [warming] Strong and laughing and certain we would conquer the world. [voice hardening] But he wasn't always that man. Sometimes he was weak. Drunk. Paralyzed. [bitter] And I had to—

Margaret Thatcher: [finishing, knowing] Carry him.

Cleopatra: [surprised, grateful] Yes.

Margaret Thatcher: [tired, heavy] I know that weight. Not with Denis—he was steady. But colleagues. Ministers who crumbled when the pressure came. [frustrated] Who needed me to be certain so they could function. [rubbing eyes, exhausted] God, it's exhausting. Being the spine for other people.

Cleopatra: [nodding, bitter] They never thank you for it.

Margaret Thatcher: [darkening, anger building] No. They resent you for seeing their weakness. [pause, cold] And then they betray you.

Cleopatra: [sharply, leaning forward] Who betrayed you?

Margaret Thatcher: [bitter, building intensity] My own cabinet. November 1990. [voice tightening] One by one, they came to my office—men I had made, men whose careers I built— [voice rising] and they told me to resign. [SHOUTING, sudden] Geoffrey Howe! [catching herself, seething] I made that man Foreign Secretary. [cold, cutting] And he destroyed me with a speech.

Cleopatra: [matter-of-fact, dark] You should have had him killed.

Margaret Thatcher: [startled laugh, genuine] We don't—we don't do that anymore.

Cleopatra: [shrugging, amused] More's the pity. [deadpan] A few executions focus the mind.

Margaret Thatcher: [still laughing, loosening] I considered metaphorical executions. [sobering] But by then it was too late. They wanted me gone. [quieter, hurt showing] They were tired of being afraid of me.

Cleopatra: [nodding slowly, understanding] This is always how it ends. They need you when there's chaos. [building] When you've created order, they resent the hand that holds the— [searching] what's your word? The whip.

Margaret Thatcher: [tired, admitting] I never learned to smile through it. That was my mistake.

Cleopatra: [warmly, teasing] It's probably why you lasted eleven years instead of my twenty-one.

Margaret Thatcher: [snorting, dry] You had the advantage of executing your rivals.

Cleopatra: [casually, chilling] I had my sister killed, yes. Arsinoe. [matter-of-fact] She raised an army against me during Caesar's war. [shrugging, cold] One can't be sentimental about sisters who do that.

Margaret Thatcher: [pause, processing] I had no sisters. Maybe that helped.

Cleopatra: [suddenly animated, excited] Oh! I've been reading your philosopher. Nietzsche. [leaning forward] In this strange afterlife there's a library—anyway, he writes about heroes. The Übermensch. [intense] Someone who creates their own values, stands beyond good and evil. [challenging] Do you think women can be this? The hero?

Margaret Thatcher: [firmly, certain] Anyone who asks that question has already answered it wrong. [sharp] Heroes aren't born from their sex. They're forged by will and circumstance.

Cleopatra: [pushing back] But the circumstances are different for us. [insistent] You can't deny this. The rules are written by men. For men.

Margaret Thatcher: [sharply, fierce] Then you rewrite the rules. [building, passionate] Or you win so completely within them that they can't exclude you without excluding victory itself.

Cleopatra: [challenging, testing] And if you can't win? If the rules are designed to make your victory impossible?

Margaret Thatcher: [pause, then quietly, intense] Then you change the game. [leaning in] You find ground where the old rules don't apply. [building, passionate] When I became party leader, they said a woman could never win a general election. So I didn't run as a woman. I ran as a conviction. As an idea. [triumphant] They couldn't vote against me without voting against everything I represented.

Cleopatra: [nodding slowly, appreciating] I did something similar. [proud] I wasn't just a queen. I was Isis incarnate. The living goddess. [fierce] Rejecting me meant rejecting the divine order of Egypt.

Margaret Thatcher: [dry, slight smile] I preferred economics to theology.

Cleopatra: [amused, teasing] You worshipped the market like I worshipped the Nile.

Margaret Thatcher: [firmly] The market rewards those who provide value. [pointed] The Nile floods regardless of virtue.

Cleopatra: [laughing, defensive] The Nile fed my people for three thousand years! [challenging] Can your market say the same?

Margaret Thatcher: [warming up, proud] The market has lifted more people from poverty in two centuries than all the pharaohs combined. [excited, animated] We have machines now—computers—that think faster than any human. Medicines that cure diseases you would have called curses. [building wonder] Machines that fly through the air carrying hundreds of people.

Cleopatra: [stunned, genuine awe] Machines that fly?

Margaret Thatcher: [enjoying this, expansive] Across oceans. In hours. [proud] I flew to the Falkland Islands—eight thousand miles—to visit our troops. In your day that journey would've taken months.

Cleopatra: [awed, leaning forward] And the common people? Do they fly?

Margaret Thatcher: [proudly, triumphant] Yes. A factory worker in Birmingham can holiday in Greece for less than a week's wages.

Cleopatra: [long pause, overwhelmed] I can't... [shaking head] in my time, the common people never left their villages. They were born, they farmed, they died. All within sight of the same temples.

Margaret Thatcher: [nodding, sobering] That world is gone. For better and worse. [souring] The people now expect things. Comfort. Entertainment. Freedom. [bitter] And they expect someone else to provide it.

Cleopatra: [sharp, agreeing] This is the danger of feeding people too well. [warning] They forget someone has to grow the grain.

Margaret Thatcher: [intensely, passionate] Exactly. This was my war. Not the Falklands—the war at home. [building, fierce] Against the idea that the state should provide everything. That people needn't work, needn't save, needn't take responsibility. [SHOUTING, passionate] I didn't fight my way to the top to build a nation of dependents!

Cleopatra: [calmly, curious] Did you win?

Margaret Thatcher: [deflating slightly, honest] I won battles. Broke the unions. Sold the state industries. [some pride] Made Britain believe in enterprise again. [quieter, tired] But the war goes on. There's always someone who wants to take from the productive and give to the idle.

Cleopatra: [philosophical, musing] Maybe this is the eternal struggle. Those who build and those who take. [pause] In my day it was Rome swallowing kingdoms. In yours it's... bureaucrats?

Margaret Thatcher: [with real venom, disgusted] Bureaucrats. Socialists. [bitter, cutting] People who've never built anything but presume to tell others how to live.

Cleopatra: [amused, observing] You hate them very much.

Margaret Thatcher: [passionate, building] I hate what they do to people. How they make them small. Dependent. Grateful for crumbs. [intense, fierce] A person's dignity comes from what they build, what they earn, what they provide. Take that away and you take away their soul.

Cleopatra: [thoughtful, different perspective] My people didn't think this way. [simply] The Nile provided. The Pharaoh distributed. This was the order for three thousand years.

Margaret Thatcher: [dismissive, cutting] And where is Egypt now? [sharp] A tourist attraction.

Cleopatra: [coldly, striking back] Where is your empire, Lady Thatcher?

Margaret Thatcher: [hit, pausing, wounded] ...Gone. Yes. [quieter, admitting] But we left something. Laws. Language. Ideas about liberty. [recovering] The Americans took what we built and—

Cleopatra: [interrupting, sharp] The Americans took what Rome built. Rome took what Greece built. Greece took what we built. [pause, wise] Empire is a relay race. You pass the torch and pretend you lit it.

Margaret Thatcher: [grudging respect, slight smile] ...That's well put.

Cleopatra: [pleased] I had a good education. [pause, then seriously, probing] Tell me truly. Was it worth it? [heavy] The power. The loneliness. The children who admire more than they love. The men who fear more than they trust.

Margaret Thatcher: [long silence, struggling] I didn't have a choice.

Cleopatra: [gently, challenging] Everyone has a choice.

Margaret Thatcher: [firmly, certain] No. [pause] Some of us are built for this. We can't be happy without it. We can't be ourselves without it. [quieter, raw] "Was it worth it" assumes there was something else I could have been. [WHISPER] There wasn't.

Cleopatra: [quietly, understanding] Yes. I understand. [remembering] I could have surrendered to Octavian. Walked in his triumph. Lived in some comfortable exile. [pause, proud] I chose the asp instead.

Margaret Thatcher: [softly, moved] You chose to die a queen.

Cleopatra: [correcting, fierce] I chose to die myself. [passionate] The cobra was sacred to us. Divine. In death I became Isis fully. [firmly, defiant] Octavian could have my kingdom. He couldn't have my surrender.

Margaret Thatcher: [moved, voice cracking] I wish I'd managed something similar. [vulnerable, breaking] Instead I wept. In the back of a car, leaving Downing Street. [WHISPER, ashamed] They saw me weep.

Cleopatra: [warmly, comforting] There's no shame in that. [gently] You lived to weep. I didn't live to show them anything but strength. [pause, sincere] Maybe your way was braver.

Margaret Thatcher: [recovering, wiping eye, laughing slightly] I doubt that. But thank you.

Cleopatra: [leaning in, intense] We're rare, you and I. [passionate] Women who held the thing itself. Not influence. Not proximity. The throne. [building] Do you know how few of us there have been? Across all of history?

Margaret Thatcher: [nodding, heavy] I know. I thought about it often. [listing, reverent] Elizabeth. Victoria. Catherine. You. [pause] A handful. Thousands of years.

Cleopatra: [bitter, angry] And they call us exceptional. Like we're accidents. Flukes. [building, furious] Like the system worked correctly and we slipped through.

Margaret Thatcher: [firmly, correcting] The system did work correctly. It produced us when it needed us.

Cleopatra: [challenging, fierce] Or we bent it until it broke.

Margaret Thatcher: [small smile, appreciating] Maybe that too.

Cleopatra: [standing, stretching, lighter] God, I've been sitting too long. [pause, musing] I think we would have been enemies. In life.

Margaret Thatcher: [also standing, agreeing] Almost certainly. [dry] You're too pragmatic. I'm too ideological.

Cleopatra: [amused, teasing] You would have called me amoral.

Margaret Thatcher: [returning serve] You would have called me naive.

Cleopatra: [extending hand, warm] And we'd both have been right.

Margaret Thatcher: [taking it, thoughtful] The Greeks had a word. Thumos. Spiritedness. The part of the soul that wants honor. Recognition.

Cleopatra: [nodding, rueful] We have too much of it. Far too much. [laughing, self-aware] It made us impossible to live with.

Margaret Thatcher: [quietly, honest] But possible to remember.

Cleopatra: [pause, moved] Yes. That's something. [building, proud] Two thousand years and they still say our names.

Margaret Thatcher: [fierce, passionate] Because we didn't ask permission. We didn't wait to be given power. [building to crescendo] We took it.

Cleopatra: [softly, honest] And we paid for it.

Margaret Thatcher: [meeting her eyes, certain] Every day. [pause] And we'd do it again.

Cleopatra: [smiling, fierce] Without hesitation.

Margaret Thatcher: [offering arm, warm] Walk with me? I find I want to keep talking.

Cleopatra: [taking it, delighted] Tell me more about the machines that fly. And these computers. [laughing, genuine] I want to know everything I missed.

Margaret Thatcher: [warmly, relaxing] It'll take a while. Quite a lot of history since you left.

Cleopatra: [amused, content] I have nothing but time now.

Margaret Thatcher: [dryly, deadpan] That's one advantage of being dead.

Cleopatra: [laughing, squeezing her arm] The only one, I think. [warm] But we'll make the most of it.
