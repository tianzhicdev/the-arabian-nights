Kublai Khan: [warm][thoughtful] Come, young Venetian. Sit closer. [gentle] The fire grows cold, but your stories warm these old bones more than any flame could. [curious] Your father and uncle... they have told me much of their first journey to my court. But I wish to hear it from you. [leaning in] You were but a boy when they returned to Venice. What do you remember?
Marco Polo: [reverent] Great Khan, I remember everything. [emotional] The night they came back... [pause] I was fifteen years old. I had never known my father. He left Venice before I was born, when my mother still carried me in her womb.
Kublai Khan: [softly][sympathetic] Ah. And your mother?
Marco Polo: [sorrowful] Dead, my lord. [pause] She died giving me life. [matter-of-fact] I was raised by relatives, always hearing whispers about my father... the merchant who vanished into the East. Some said he was dead. Others said he had found riches beyond imagination. [quietly] I believed neither. I simply... waited.
Kublai Khan: [thoughtfully][impressed] Patience. The rarest virtue. [nodding] Continue.
Marco Polo: [warming to the story] The year was 1250 when my father Nicolo and my uncle Maffeo first set sail from Venice. They were traders... respectable men, the kind who measure success in bolts of silk and bags of spices. [proudly] Constantinople was their first stop. You know of Constantinople, Great Khan?
Kublai Khan: [knowingly] The capital of the Romans in the West. [wryly] I have heard it described as a city of churches and intrigue.
Marco Polo: [laughs] A perfect description. [informative] At that time, a Venetian magistrate governed part of the city... we had conquered it fifty years earlier, alongside the French. My father saw opportunity there, but greater opportunity lay beyond. [building excitement] They decided to sail into the Black Sea.
Kublai Khan: [nodding] The Euxine. [reflective] My grandfather Genghis spoke of the lands beyond those waters.
Marco Polo: [enthusiastic] Indeed! They made port at Soldaia... a trading post on the Crimean peninsula where merchants from a dozen nations haggled over furs and slaves. [conspiratorial] But my father was ambitious. He had purchased jewels... beautiful, costly jewels... and he wished to find a buyer who could pay what they were truly worth.
Kublai Khan: [knowing smile] So they sought out my cousin Barka.
Marco Polo: [excited] Yes! Barka Khan, lord of the Western Tartars. [dramatic] They rode for many days across grasslands that seemed to stretch into eternity. [reflective] I've since traveled those same routes, and I understand now what courage it took... two Venetians, alone, venturing into the unknown with nothing but their wits and a saddlebag of gemstones.
Kublai Khan: [amused][teasing] Courage? [pause] Or perhaps naivety?
Marco Polo: [laughing warmly] Perhaps both, Great Khan. [continuing] But fortune favors the bold. Barka received them with great honor. He was charmed by these strange Europeans who spoke Tartar...
Kublai Khan: [surprised][impressed] They learned the language?
Marco Polo: [proudly] They had to survive, my lord. A merchant who cannot speak is a merchant who cannot sell. [building] When my father presented the jewels to Barka, the Khan was so pleased that he paid double their worth and gave them additional gifts besides.
Kublai Khan: [reflective][slightly melancholy] Barka was always generous. Too generous, some said. [pause] It made him many friends and nearly as many enemies.
Marco Polo: [growing serious] Those enemies would soon matter greatly. [darkening tone] My father and uncle had lived at Barka's court for a year when war broke out... a fierce war between Barka and another great lord, Hulagu of the Eastern Tartars.
Kublai Khan: [voice dropping][heavy] My brother Hulagu. [pause] He was relentless in battle.
Marco Polo: [grave] The roads became rivers of blood. There was no safe passage back to Constantinople. [tense] My father and uncle were trapped. They consulted with merchants and scholars, trying to find any route home. [curious] Someone suggested they travel eastward... to skirt around the war zones entirely.
Kublai Khan: [surprised][intrigued] Eastward? To return west, they went east?
Marco Polo: [laughs] It sounds mad, doesn't it? [thoughtful] But sometimes the longest path is the only path. [descriptive] They traveled to a town called Oukaka, then crossed a great river... the Tigris, they called it, though I believe now it was the Sihun. [pause][dramatic] And then came the desert.
Kublai Khan: [leaning forward][curious] Ah. The desert. Tell me of it.
Marco Polo: [awe][slow and deliberate] Seventeen days of nothing, Great Khan. [quiet] No cities, no castles, no buildings of any kind. [descriptive] Only Tartar herdsmen with their tents and their flocks, moving like ghosts across the sand. [intense] The sun burned by day, and the cold bit by night. [wondrous] My father said he saw mirages... cities of gold that dissolved as he approached. [serious] He learned to trust nothing his eyes told him.
Kublai Khan: [wise][knowing] The desert is a teacher. [pause] It strips away everything false.
Marco Polo: [continuing][building hope] At last, they reached Bokhara... a noble city, the finest in all of Persia. [pause][sigh] And here, fate intervened. They could go no further. The roads were blocked, the dangers too great. [resigned] For three years, they remained in Bokhara, learning, trading, waiting.
Kublai Khan: [reflective] Three years. [thoughtful] A man can change entirely in such time.
Marco Polo: [passionate] My father was not the same man who left Venice. He had learned three languages. He understood the ways of the Tartars... their laws, their customs, their beliefs. [building excitement] And then, in Bokhara, he met your ambassador.
Kublai Khan: [leaning forward][remembering] I remember receiving the report. [nodding] An ambassador returning from my brother Hulagu, speaking of two remarkable Europeans he had encountered.
Marco Polo: [excited] The ambassador told my father that you... the great Kublai Khan, supreme lord of all the Tartars... had never seen a European. That you were curious about our lands, our kings, our religion. [pause] He invited them to join his caravan.
Kublai Khan: [simply] And they accepted.
Marco Polo: [rhetorical] How could they refuse? The alternative was to remain in Bokhara indefinitely, or to risk death returning through the war zones. [epic tone] So they set out... northward and eastward, into lands no Venetian had ever seen. [dramatic] The journey took an entire year, Great Khan. An entire year of mountains that scraped the sky, of rivers swollen with snowmelt, of cold that turned a man's breath to ice before it left his lips.
Kublai Khan: [satisfied] But they reached me.
Marco Polo: [warm][admiring] They reached you. And you received them with such warmth, such curiosity! [animated] My father described those first audiences... how you asked about the Pope, about our kings, about the Christ we worship. [impressed] You wanted to understand everything.
Kublai Khan: [thoughtful][passionate] I still do. Knowledge is power, young Marco. [wise] But knowledge of distant lands is a different kind of power... it is the power to imagine what might be. [remembering] Your father told me of cathedrals that took centuries to build. Of universities where men studied the wisdom of the ancients. Of ships that crossed vast oceans guided by the stars.
Marco Polo: [quietly][significantly] And you asked for missionaries.
Kublai Khan: [nodding slowly] One hundred learned men. [explaining] Men who could explain Christianity, who could debate with my Buddhist priests and my Taoist scholars. Men who could teach the arts and sciences of Europe. [firmly] I was not seeking conversion... I was seeking wisdom.
Marco Polo: [understanding] My father understood this. [formal] You gave them letters for the Pope, written in the Tartar tongue. [reverent] And you gave them something far more precious... the golden tablet.
Kublai Khan: [proud][authoritative] The tablet that bears my seal. Any who carry it may travel freely through all my lands, demand provisions and escorts, and speak with my voice.
Marco Polo: [grateful][serious] That tablet saved their lives, Great Khan. More than once. [darkening] The journey back was even more perilous than the journey forward. A Tartar officer accompanied them, but he fell ill after only twenty days and had to be left behind. [tense] My father and uncle pressed on alone... across mountains, through hostile territories, in the depths of winter.
Kublai Khan: [curious] How long?
Marco Polo: [sighs][exhausted tone] Three years. Three years to reach the Mediterranean. [relieved] They arrived at Laiassus, in Armenia, and sailed to Acre. [pause][disappointed] And there they learned terrible news.
Kublai Khan: [knowing] The Pope had died.
Marco Polo: [frustrated] Pope Clement IV, yes. And no new Pope had been chosen. [disgusted] The Church was paralyzed by politics and ambition. The legate they found in Acre... a man named Tebaldo... advised them to wait.
Kublai Khan: [matter-of-fact] And so they waited.
Marco Polo: [growing agitated] For two years they waited, Great Khan. [anxious] Two years, growing older, growing anxious. [urgent] Finally, they could wait no longer. They feared you would think they had abandoned their mission... or worse, that you would think they had died. [determined] So they returned to Acre, collected what holy oil they could from the lamp at Christ's sepulchre, and prepared to return to you. [pause][meaningful] This time, they brought me with them.
Kublai Khan: [with genuine warmth][smiling] The young Marco. How old were you?
Marco Polo: [young and eager] Seventeen, my lord. Old enough to travel, young enough to believe I was immortal. [nostalgic] I remember standing on the dock, watching Venice grow smaller behind us. [quietly] I wondered if I would ever see it again.
Kublai Khan: [curious][probing] Did you fear the journey?
Marco Polo: [pausing][thoughtful] I feared... missing it. [passionate] Does that make sense? My father had seen things I could only dream of. He had stood in the presence of the greatest ruler on earth. He had crossed deserts and mountains and seas. [rhetorical][frustrated] How could I sit in Venice, counting coins and measuring cloth, when such wonders existed?
Kublai Khan: [quietly][moved] You have your father's spirit.
Marco Polo: [excited][building] We had barely left Armenia when fate smiled upon us again. The legate Tebaldo... the same man who had advised my father to wait... was elected Pope! [triumphant] He took the name Gregory the Tenth and immediately sent messengers to summon us back to Acre.
Kublai Khan: [hopeful] At last, your mission could be fulfilled.
Marco Polo: [deflating][disappointed] Partially fulfilled, Great Khan. [sighs] Pope Gregory could not send one hundred learned men. The Church was too fractured, the journey too dangerous. [underwhelmed] He sent two... two Dominican friars, men of books and theology. And he sent letters, and gifts of crystal, and his blessing.
Kublai Khan: [flatly][unimpressed] Two friars. [pause] From one hundred to two.
Marco Polo: [bitter] Even those two did not complete the journey. [frustrated] When we reached Armenia again, we learned that the Sultan of Egypt... Bundokdari, the Mamelukes called him... had invaded with a vast army. [contemptuous] The friars were terrified. They handed their letters and gifts to my father and fled back to the coast under the protection of the Knights Templar.
Kublai Khan: [with a trace of contempt] Men of God who feared for their lives.
Marco Polo: [defensive but understanding] Can you blame them, Great Khan? They were scholars, not soldiers. They had never seen war, never felt the ground shake beneath cavalry charges. [proud] My father and uncle... they had become hard men by then. Hard and determined. Nothing would stop them from completing their mission.
Kublai Khan: [direct][interested] And you?
Marco Polo: [honest][vulnerable] I was a boy pretending to be a man. [determined] But I had inherited my father's stubbornness. [epic][descriptive] We pressed on... through Armenia, through Persia, through lands where the names meant nothing to me but the dangers were very real. [listing hardships] Bandits. Illness. Storms that buried us in snow. [dramatic] Once, we were lost for a week in a mountain pass, surviving on horse meat and melted ice.
Kublai Khan: [respectful] Yet you survived.
Marco Polo: [exhausted but proud] Three and a half years, Great Khan. Three and a half years to reach your court. [transformed] I was no longer the boy who left Venice. [dark] I had seen death. I had killed... once, a bandit who tried to steal our horses. [hardened] I had learned to sleep on stone and eat food that would have made me retch as a child.
Kublai Khan: [rising slowly][formal] And now you are here. In my palace. Speaking my language. Serving me as my father and uncle once served me.
Marco Polo: [humble][sincere] I am honored beyond words, Great Khan.
Kublai Khan: [thoughtful] Honored? Perhaps. [meaningful][building] But let me tell you something, young Marco. When your father first stood before me, I saw a merchant. A clever merchant, resourceful and brave, but still a merchant. [pause] When you stand before me now, I see something different.
Marco Polo: [curious][hopeful] What do you see, my lord?
Kublai Khan: [profound][visionary] A bridge. Between my world and yours. Between the sunrise and the sunset. [dismissive] Your father brought me trinkets and stories. [emphatic] You... you bring me understanding. You learn quickly. You observe everything. You write it all down.
Marco Polo: [earnest] I wish to remember, Great Khan. I wish to understand.
Kublai Khan: [expansive][excited] Then I will send you places no European has ever been. I will show you my empire... all of it. [listing with growing passion] The silk roads and the spice islands. The mountains of Tibet and the jungles of Burma. The great wall that keeps out the northern barbarians and the cities of the south that make Venice look like a fishing village.
Marco Polo: [pledging][solemn] I will serve you faithfully, Great Khan.
Kublai Khan: [sitting back down][curious][testing] One more question, Marco. That lamp in Jerusalem... the one with the holy oil. What does it mean to your people?
Marco Polo: [reverent][explaining] It burns above the tomb where we believe Christ was buried and from which he rose again. [spiritual] The oil is sacred... touched by the presence of God himself.
Kublai Khan: [pointed][challenging] And yet your Pope... the highest priest of your faith... sent only two frightened scholars to represent him to the greatest empire on earth?
Marco Polo: [after a long pause][careful][diplomatic] I cannot speak for the Pope, Great Khan. I can only speak for myself.
Kublai Khan: [pressing] And what do you say?
Marco Polo: [honest][critical] I say that sometimes great empires become blind to what lies beyond their borders. [building conviction] They forget that there are other worlds, other ways of seeing. The Pope is consumed by wars with other Christians, by power struggles within his own church. He cannot see...
Kublai Khan: [interrupting][finishing the thought] He cannot see that the real world is larger than his small kingdom.
Marco Polo: [agreeing] Yes, Great Khan.
Kublai Khan: [standing][decisive][commanding] Then we must teach him. And we will start with you, Marco Polo. [passionate] You will learn everything I can teach you. And someday... many years from now... you will return to your Venice and tell them what you have seen. [prophetic] You will be my ambassador to the future.
Marco Polo: [solemn][determined] I will not fail you, Great Khan.
Kublai Khan: [a rare smile][warm][confident] No. [pause] I do not believe you will. [lighter tone] Now come... there is a feast tonight in honor of your arrival. My generals wish to meet these strange Europeans who walked across the world to find me.
Marco Polo: [narrator voice][reflective][to the audience] And so began my seventeen years in the service of Kublai Khan. [wonder] Seventeen years of wonders that I will spend the rest of my life trying to describe. [pause][mysterious][promising] But that, dear listener, is a story for another time.
