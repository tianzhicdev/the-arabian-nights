Hope in the Proles - Episode Seven

[pause-3]

'If there is hope,' wrote Winston in his diary, 'it lies in the proles.'

[pause]

The words echoed in his mind as he gazed out his window at the vast proletarian quarters of London. If hope existed anywhere, it had to be there - in those sprawling neighborhoods where eighty-five percent of Oceania's population lived their lives.

[pause-2]

The proles, those masses deemed insignificant by the Party, lived in a different world than Party members. Their lives were simpler, more primitive, but perhaps... more human.

[pause]

Winston remembered the old man in the pub, his fragmented memories of life before the Revolution. Those memories, incomplete as they were, represented something precious - a link to the past that the Party couldn't fully erase.

[pause-2]

Looking down at his diary, Winston continued writing: "Until they become conscious they will never rebel, and until after they have rebelled they cannot become conscious."

[pause-3]

The truth of this paradox weighed heavily on him. The proles had the strength to shake off the Party like a horse shaking off flies. If they could only realize their power... if they could only wake up...

[pause-2]

But the Party kept them docile with simple pleasures - cheap beer, gambling, football, and tawdry fiction. Their world was one of primitive emotions, basic needs, and daily struggles.

[pause]

Winston thought about Julia's words: "The proles are human beings. We are not human."

[pause-3]

Perhaps that was the heart of it. In their very degradation, the proles had preserved something essential that Party members had lost. Their feelings were genuine, their relationships real, their pleasures - however basic - were their own.

[pause-2]

The question remained: Could hope truly lie in those who didn't even know they were oppressed?

[pause-3]

Only time would tell. And time, in Oceania, was something no one could truly measure anymore.
