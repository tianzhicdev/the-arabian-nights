# The Lizzie Borden Murders
# A True Crime Narration
# Runtime: Approximately 20 minutes

# ============================================================
# COLD OPEN (30-45 seconds)
# ============================================================

August 4th, 1892.

Fall River, Massachusetts.

[pause-2]

A scream cuts through the summer heat.

"Maggie! Come down! Come down quick!"

[pause-2]

"Father's dead. Somebody came in and killed him."

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# INTRODUCTION
# ============================================================

This is the story of Lizzie Borden.

[pause-2]

A Sunday school teacher.

A member of the Woman's Christian Temperance Union.

A respectable spinster of thirty-two years.

[pause-2]

And the prime suspect in one of the most brutal murders in American history.

[pause-3]

Lizzie Borden took an axe,
And gave her mother forty whacks.
When she saw what she had done,
She gave her father forty-one.

[pause-2]

That's the rhyme every schoolchild knows.

But the truth is stranger.

Darker.

And to this day, unsolved.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART ONE: THE HOUSE ON SECOND STREET
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Ninety-two Second Street.

A modest Greek Revival house in a cramped neighborhood of Fall River.

[pause-2]

This was strange.

Andrew Jackson Borden was one of the wealthiest men in the city.

President of a bank.

Director of several mills.

Worth somewhere between two hundred and three hundred thousand dollars.

[pause-2]

In today's money, that's roughly ten million.

[pause-3]

Yet Andrew Borden refused to live on the Hill, where the other wealthy families built their mansions.

He refused to install indoor plumbing.

He refused to connect the house to the new electric lines.

[pause-2]

Instead, chamber pots sat under every bed.

Kerosene lamps lit the rooms.

And the family used an outhouse in the basement.

[pause-3]

Andrew Borden was a miser.

And everyone in Fall River knew it.

[pause-2]

The house itself was peculiar.

Originally built as a two-family dwelling, Andrew had converted it—but not well.

[pause-2]

The upstairs had no connecting hallway.

The front stairs led to the daughters' rooms and the guest room.

The back stairs led to the parents' bedroom and the maid's quarters.

[pause-2]

To get from one side to the other, you had to go downstairs, cross the house, and climb back up.

[pause-3]

The Bordens lived like strangers under the same roof.

And in many ways, they were.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART TWO: THE FAMILY
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Andrew Borden had married twice.

His first wife, Sarah, gave him three daughters.

One died in infancy.

Two survived: Emma and Lizzie.

[pause-2]

Sarah died when Lizzie was not quite three years old.

[pause-3]

Three years later, Andrew married Abby Durfee Gray.

A short, stout, quiet woman.

The girls never accepted her.

[pause-2]

Lizzie refused to call her "Mother."

She called her "Mrs. Borden."

[pause-2]

When asked about this at the inquest, Lizzie was blunt.

"She is not my mother. She is my stepmother. My mother is dead."

[pause-3]

The household tension had a clear source: money.

[pause-2]

In the months before the murders, Andrew had given his wife's sister a house.

Lizzie and Emma were furious.

[pause-2]

They demanded compensation.

Their father gave them a rental property—the house where they had lived as children.

He sold it to them for one dollar.

[pause-3]

But the resentment festered.

[pause-2]

The sisters rarely ate meals with their parents.

They communicated through the maid, Bridget Sullivan.

They kept to their side of the house.

[pause-2]

In May of 1892, Andrew killed a flock of pigeons in the barn.

He said they attracted boys who might break into the property.

[pause-2]

Lizzie had built a roost for those pigeons.

She was said to be devastated.

[pause-3]

Her father had killed them with a hatchet.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART THREE: THE DAY BEFORE
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Wednesday, August 3rd.

[pause-2]

Lizzie's uncle, John Morse, arrived for a visit.

He was the brother of Andrew's first wife.

[pause-2]

That evening, something was wrong in the Borden house.

Andrew and Abby were violently ill.

Vomiting.

Stomach cramps.

[pause-2]

They blamed it on the mutton stew.

It had been reheated several times in the summer heat.

[pause-3]

But Lizzie visited her friend Alice Russell that night.

What she said was strange.

[pause-2]

She told Alice that the family had been sick.

That their milk might have been poisoned.

That she had seen a strange man lurking around the house.

[pause-2]

"I feel as if something were hanging over me," she said.

"I cannot throw it off."

[pause-3]

She spoke of her father's enemies.

His tenants.

People he had wronged in business.

[pause-2]

"I am afraid somebody will do something," she said.

[pause-3]

The next day, her father would be dead.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART FOUR: THE MORNING OF AUGUST 4TH
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Thursday.

The hottest day of the year so far.

[pause-2]

Bridget Sullivan, the maid, woke at six-fifteen with a headache.

She had been sick too.

[pause-2]

She went downstairs.

Unlocked the back door.

Took in the milk.

Started breakfast.

[pause-3]

By seven o'clock, Andrew, Abby, and John Morse were eating in the dining room.

Mutton soup.

Mutton.

Johnnycakes.

Coffee.

Bananas.

Cookies.

[pause-2]

The same mutton that had made them sick the day before.

[pause-3]

Lizzie came down late, around nine.

She said she wasn't hungry.

She would have coffee and cookies.

[pause-2]

John Morse left around quarter to nine to visit relatives.

Andrew left shortly after to make his rounds downtown.

[pause-3]

That left three people in the house.

Abby.

Lizzie.

And Bridget.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART FIVE: THE FIRST MURDER
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Around nine o'clock, Abby asked Bridget to wash the windows.

[pause-2]

It was brutally hot work.

Bridget went outside with her pail and her squeegee.

She spent the next hour and a half washing the windows on the outside of the house.

[pause-3]

At some point during this time, Abby went upstairs.

To the guest room.

To change the pillowcases on the bed where John Morse had slept.

[pause-2]

She never came back down.

[pause-3]

Someone came up behind her.

Someone with a hatchet.

[pause-2]

Nineteen blows.

To the back of her head.

To her neck.

To her shoulders.

[pause-2]

Abby Borden fell face-first onto the floor.

Between the bed and the dresser.

She died on her hands and knees.

[pause-3]

The blood soaked into the carpet.

But not far.

Abby was a large woman.

Her body blocked most of the splatter.

[pause-2]

The killer would have been covered in blood.

Yet somehow, no one saw a thing.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART SIX: THE SECOND MURDER
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Around ten forty-five, Andrew Borden returned home.

[pause-2]

This is where things get strange.

[pause-3]

The front door was locked.

Not just the spring lock.

All three locks.

Bolted and keyed from the inside.

[pause-2]

Bridget had to struggle to open it.

She muttered under her breath.

"Oh, pshaw."

[pause-3]

And from upstairs, she heard Lizzie laugh.

[pause-2]

Think about that.

Abby's body was lying in the guest room.

And Lizzie laughed.

[pause-3]

Andrew came inside.

He sat down in the sitting room.

He lay on the sofa.

[pause-2]

Lizzie came downstairs.

She told him that Abby had received a note—someone was sick—and she had gone out.

[pause-3]

Bridget finished the windows around eleven.

She went upstairs to rest.

To her room on the third floor.

[pause-2]

She lay down.

She may have dozed.

[pause-3]

And then she heard Lizzie calling.

"Maggie! Come down quick! Father's dead! Somebody came in and killed him!"

[pause-2]

Andrew Borden lay on the sitting room sofa.

His face destroyed.

Ten or eleven blows.

[pause-2]

One had split his eyeball cleanly in two.

He had been asleep when the first blow fell.

[pause-3]

The estimated time between the two murders?

Ninety minutes.

[pause-2]

For an hour and a half, the killer waited.

In that small, hot house.

With Abby's body cooling upstairs.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART SEVEN: THE INVESTIGATION
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

The police arrived within minutes.

Doctors.

Neighbors.

Reporters.

[pause-2]

The Borden house became chaos.

[pause-3]

The police asked Lizzie where she had been during her father's murder.

Her answer was strange.

[pause-2]

She said she was in the barn.

In the loft.

Looking for lead sinkers for a fishing trip.

[pause-2]

But it was the hottest day of the year.

The barn loft was sweltering.

Over a hundred degrees.

[pause-2]

And when police checked, they found no footprints in the dust on the floor.

[pause-3]

They asked about Abby.

Lizzie said she didn't know where she was.

[pause-2]

Then Bridget and a neighbor went upstairs.

They found Abby.

Face down.

Blood pooled around her head.

[pause-3]

She had been dead for hours.

[pause-2]

How could Lizzie not know?

How could she not have heard?

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART EIGHT: THE EVIDENCE
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

In the basement, police found several hatchets and axes.

One stood out.

[pause-2]

A hatchet head with a broken handle.

The break looked fresh.

And the head was covered in ash.

[pause-2]

As if someone had rolled it in the ashes of the stove.

To make it look old.

To hide the blood.

[pause-3]

The handle, prosecutors would argue, had been removed because it would have been soaked in blood.

[pause-2]

It was never found.

[pause-3]

But the most damning evidence came four days after the murders.

[pause-2]

Sunday morning.

Alice Russell walked into the kitchen.

[pause-2]

Lizzie was standing at the stove.

She had a dress in her hands.

A light blue dress with a dark blue figure.

[pause-2]

Emma stood at the sink.

"What are you going to do?" Emma asked.

[pause-2]

"I am going to burn this old thing up," Lizzie said.

"It is covered with paint."

[pause-3]

Alice Russell said nothing.

But later, she would testify.

[pause-2]

She told Lizzie, "I am afraid the worst thing you could have done was to burn that dress."

[pause-2]

And Lizzie replied:

"Oh, what made you let me do it? Why didn't you tell me?"

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART NINE: THE TRIAL
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Lizzie Borden was arrested on August 11th.

She pleaded not guilty.

[pause-2]

The trial began in June of 1893.

It was a sensation.

[pause-2]

Reporters came from across the country.

Every day, crowds gathered outside the New Bedford courthouse.

[pause-3]

The prosecution laid out its case.

Lizzie was the only person in the house with both victims.

Her alibi was implausible.

Her story changed repeatedly.

She had burned a dress days after the murders.

[pause-2]

And she had tried to buy poison the day before.

[pause-3]

A druggist named Eli Bence testified that Lizzie had come to his store on August 3rd.

She asked for ten cents worth of prussic acid.

[pause-2]

She said she needed it to clean a sealskin cape.

[pause-2]

The druggist refused.

Prussic acid was deadly poison.

[pause-3]

But the judges ruled this testimony inadmissible.

[pause-2]

The defense argued that no respectable woman of Lizzie's standing could commit such a crime.

[pause-2]

George Robinson, her lead attorney, was a former governor of Massachusetts.

He told the jury:

"To find her guilty, you must believe she is a fiend."

"Does she look it?"

[pause-3]

The all-male jury deliberated for ninety minutes.

They returned with their verdict.

[pause-2]

Not guilty.

[pause-3]

Lizzie walked out of the courthouse a free woman.

"I am the happiest woman in the world," she told reporters.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# PART TEN: AFTERMATH
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

Lizzie inherited her father's fortune.

She bought a mansion on the Hill.

The neighborhood she had always wanted to live in.

[pause-2]

She named it Maplecroft.

She changed her name to Lizbeth.

She tried to start over.

[pause-3]

But Fall River never forgave her.

[pause-2]

The whispers followed her everywhere.

The stares.

The rhyme that children sang.

[pause-2]

She was acquitted by the law.

But convicted by the public.

[pause-3]

Her sister Emma moved in with her.

But in 1905, Emma left.

They never spoke again.

[pause-2]

No one knows why.

[pause-3]

Lizzie Borden died on June 1st, 1927.

She was sixty-six years old.

[pause-2]

Emma died nine days later.

[pause-2]

They are buried together in Oak Grove Cemetery.

Next to their father.

And their stepmother.

[pause-5]

# ============================================================
# CLOSING REFLECTION
# ============================================================

[pause-3]

So did Lizzie Borden kill her father and stepmother?

[pause-2]

No one else was ever charged.

No other suspect was ever named.

The case remains officially unsolved.

[pause-3]

The evidence against her is circumstantial.

But it is overwhelming.

[pause-2]

She was the only one home.

Her alibi made no sense.

She burned a dress.

She laughed while her stepmother's body lay upstairs.

[pause-2]

She had motive.

She had means.

She had opportunity.

[pause-3]

And yet.

[pause-2]

The jury could not believe that a respectable Christian woman—

A Sunday school teacher—

Could take a hatchet and destroy two people.

[pause-2]

They could not believe it.

So they did not.

[pause-3]

Perhaps that is the real horror of the Lizzie Borden case.

[pause-2]

Not that someone could commit such violence.

But that they could do it—

And walk away.

[pause-2]

Smile for the cameras.

Buy a mansion.

Live another thirty-five years.

[pause-3]

And take the truth with them to the grave.

[pause-5]
