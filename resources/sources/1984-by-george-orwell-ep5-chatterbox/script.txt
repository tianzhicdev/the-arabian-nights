Part Five: Syme and the New Dictionary

[pause-3]

Deep underground, in the low-ceilinged canteen, the lunch queue inches forward with painful slowness. The stale air carries the metallic scent of boiled cabbage and the acrid smell of Victory Coffee.

[pause]

Winston's tray clatters against the greasy metal rail as he slides it along. His eyes scan the crowded room, searching for an empty seat among the sea of blue overalls.

[pause-2]

The noise is deafening - a cacophony of voices bouncing off concrete walls, mixing with the clatter of cutlery and the hiss of the steam tables. But even here, Winston knows, the telescreen's watchful eye never blinks.

[pause]

Syme waves from a table near the wall. Winston hesitates for a moment before joining him. Brilliant but dangerous - that's Syme. A specialist working on the Eleventh Edition of the Newspeak Dictionary, he speaks with an almost disturbing enthusiasm about his work.

[pause-2]

"The Eleventh Edition will be magnificent," Syme declares, his eyes gleaming with fervor. "We're destroying words - dozens of them, hundreds of them, every day."

[pause]

Winston watches silently as Syme tears into his lunch with scholarly intensity, never losing the thread of his passion for linguistic destruction.

[pause-2]

"Don't you see that the whole aim of Newspeak is to narrow the range of thought? In the end, we shall make thoughtcrime literally impossible, because there will be no words in which to express it."

[pause-3]

Looking at Syme's thin, dark face, animated with enthusiasm, Winston feels a strange certainty: within two years, Syme will be vaporized. He's too intelligent. He sees too clearly and speaks too plainly.

[pause-2]

The Party wants no such servants.

[pause-3]

End of Part Five.
